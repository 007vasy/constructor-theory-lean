# Mapping to the source papers

A constructor theorist should be able to check this file in about half an hour.
Each row is one Lean item in this repository, the sentence it encodes, and a
short faithfulness note.

Lean names are inside `namespace ConstructorTheory`. There are no Lean
`axiom` declarations. A **hypothesis** is a `Prop` that theorems may assume.

## What was read

Quotations are transcribed from the arXiv PDFs as served on 21 November 2026:

- David Deutsch, “Constructor theory”, arXiv:1210.7439v2 (17 January 2013).
  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.7439.pdf>
- David Deutsch and Chiara Marletto, “Constructor theory of information”,
  arXiv:1405.5563v2 (14 July 2014).
  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.5563.pdf>

`pdftotext` supplied the text. Quotations join line breaks and drop running
headers. Words are not corrected. A passage that was too garbled to transcribe
is not quoted. Text not inside quotation marks is a paraphrase and is marked
as one. Section numbers are the numbered headings in those PDFs.

## Vocabulary

| Lean | Kind | Source sentence | Faithfulness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Substrate` | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “The other is the system being transformed, which may consist of one or more subsystems, the substrates”. Deutsch 2013, §1.1, arXiv:1210.7439: “I shall call those physical systems the constructor’s substrates”. | Matches the role: the system acted on. Simplifies a substrate to the Lean type of its states, so a particular object’s identity is not data. Open: Deutsch 2013, §1.1, says it may be that “ultimately substrates, states and transformations would be understood in terms of tasks, not vice versa.” This formalization does the reverse. |
| `Attribute` | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “By ‘attribute’ we mean anything about the substrate that can possibly be changed by a physical process. We represent it formally as the set of states in which the substrate has that attribute.” | The second sentence is matched: an attribute is a `Set` of states. The first sentence is not enforced. Every set is an attribute here, including the empty set and sets no process can change. Deutsch 2013, §1.1, still takes the pairs in a task to be states, not attributes. |
| `Task` | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “In general, a task A is a set of ordered pairs of intrinsic attributes of some substrates: A = {x₁ → y₁, x₂ → y₂,…}.” Deutsch 2013, §1.1, arXiv:1210.7439: “I provisionally define a construction task (or ‘task’, for short) as a set of pairs such as (2), each designating a legitimate input state for the task and associating that with a legitimate output state for that input.” | Matches the 2015 set-of-pairs shape, with attributes rather than the 2013 states. “Intrinsic” is not enforced. Several outputs for one input are allowed, as in Deutsch 2013, §1.1: for `{p → q, q → r, q → s}` the constructor “may produce either r or s if presented with q”. |
| `In` / `Out` | not named | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “We call the {xᵢ} = In(A) the legitimate input attributes of A and the {yᵢ} = Out(A) its legitimate output attributes.” | Paraphrase of the recovery: the two projections of `Task.pairs`. There is no named definition. Behaviour off `In(A)` is unconstrained because pairs outside the task are not required, matching the parenthetical in the next row. |
| `Theory.Performs` | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “A constructor is capable of performing a task A if, whenever presented with substrates having an attribute in In(A), it delivers them with one of the corresponding attributes from Out(A) (regardless of what it does if the substrate is in any other state).” Deutsch 2013, §1.1, states the same clause with states instead of attributes. | `T.pairs ⊆ performs C` is the inclusion reading of “delivers one of the corresponding attributes”. Presentation, delivery, and the physical process are absent. The witness is a set of pairs. |
| `Theory` (`Constructor`, `performs`) | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “One is the object causing the transformation, which we refer to as the constructor, and whose defining characteristic is that it remains unchanged in its ability to cause the transformation again.” Deutsch 2013, §1.1, arXiv:1210.7439: a constructor is “anything that can cause transformations in physical systems without undergoing any net change in its ability to do so”, and the definition “requires it to be a constructor for A again after performing an instance of A. Its other attributes may change, but what the user must do in order to cause it to perform the task must remain the same.” | Reuse is the fact that `Performs` does not consume the witness. The constructor is not a substrate, so it has no attribute that could change, and there is no user. Open: Deutsch 2013, §3.14, treats imperfect constructors as substrates of tasks of the form “cause C to perform Aε and to remain capable of doing so again.” That task is not formalized. The epsilon in the PDF is a separate symbol after A. |
| `Theory.Possible` | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563, repeated from Deutsch 2013, §1.1. “But we call a task A possible (which we write as A✓) if the laws of nature impose no limit, short of perfection, on how accurately A could be performed, nor on how well things that are capable of approximately performing it could retain their ability to do so again.” | `Possible` is “some constructor’s pair-set contains the task”, read as the exact limit. There is no accuracy measure and no sequence of approximations. The same paragraph of the 2015 paper requires subsidiary theories to “provide a measure of the accuracy” and “a meaning to whether an infinite sequence of tasks A₁, A₂, … converges to a limiting task A”. That measure is not formalized. Deutsch 2013, §3.14, introduces tasks Aε; those are not formalized either. |
| `Theory.Impossible` | definition | Same sentence as `Possible`, continuing: “Otherwise A is impossible (which we write as A✘).” | Matched as the negation of `Possible`. Nothing is added. |
| `Task.serial` (`⊚`) | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “When Out(A) = In(B), the serial composition BA is the task whose net effect is that of performing A and then B on the same substrate.” Deutsch 2013, §1.2, arXiv:1210.7439: “The same holds for the serial composition AB, which is defined such that in cases where every legitimate output state of B is a legitimate input state of A, AB is the task of achieving the net effect of performing B and then immediately performing A on the same substrate. (Other cases will not concern us in this paper.)” | The net effect “one task, then the other” is matched. Letter order is not: our `A ⊚ B` performs `A` then `B`, which is the papers’ `BA`, not their `AB`. “Immediately” is not represented. The papers define serial composition only when the outputs of the first task are the inputs of the second, and set other cases aside. Our `serial` is relational composition for every pair of tasks: a pair survives when some intermediate attribute matches. Open: restrict `⊚` to the case `Out = In`. |
| `Task.parallel` (`⊗`) | definition | Deutsch 2013, §1.2, arXiv:1210.7439. “The parallel composition A ⊗ B of tasks A and B is the task of achieving the effect of performing A on one substrate and B on another.” Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2: “the task whose net effect on a composite system M ⊕ N is that of performing A on M and B on N.” | Matched as a task on the product type whose pairs are Cartesian products of the factors’ attributes. The papers also say performing `A ⊗ B` “need not involve actually performing A or B”. Our pair-set does not require the factors to have been performed. It does not, by itself, allow a parallel task whose effect is not the product of two attribute-transformations. |
| `Task.transpose` (`ᵀ`) | definition | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “The transpose of a task A = {x₁ → y₁, x₂ → y₂, …} is A~ = {y₁ → x₁, y₂ → x₂, …}.” Deutsch 2013, §1.1: “The transpose A~ of A is the task with all the input and output states of A swapped, so {p → q, q → r, q → s}~ ≡ {q → p, r → q, s → q}.” | Matched, on attributes (2015) rather than states (2013). |
| `Task.union` | definition | Deutsch 2013, §3.4, arXiv:1210.7439. Subsidiary theories “would also be required to define a relation of union between tasks (corresponding to set-theoretic union in the notation of this paper).” | `union` is that set-theoretic union of pair-sets. The requirement that every subsidiary theory supply some union operation is not formalized; this development fixes one. |
| `Task.IsSubtask` (`⊑`) | definition | No definition is stated. Deutsch 2013, §3.4, uses “proper subtasks”, and §1.2 speaks of “networks of sub-tasks”. Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §8.9, arXiv:1405.5563, speak of “the subtask with legitimate input set v”. | Paraphrase, marked as such: since a task is a set of pairs and union is set-theoretic union, a subtask is read as a subset of pairs. That includes the 2015 use (restrict the legitimate inputs) and is broader: any pairs may be dropped, not only by shrinking the input set. Open: the papers may mean only input-restrictions. |
| `Task.hasDisjointInputs` | definition | Not stated for tasks. Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563: “Any set of disjoint attributes, we shall call a physical variable.” | This predicate is ours. It is not the definition of a variable, and neither paper says that the input attributes of a task must be disjoint. `Possible` does not imply it. |
| `Task.isFunctional` | definition | Not stated. The contrary is allowed by Deutsch 2013, §1.1, quoted under `Task`. | Ours. It cuts out tasks the 2013 example explicitly permits. Theorems about it are conditional on a property the papers do not assume. |
| `Task.empty`, `Task.idOn` | definitions | Not stated. | Ours, so that serial composition has an absorber and a unit. `idOn Set.univ` contains `(x, x)` for every attribute `x`, which is a much larger task than a single identity branch. |
| `Task.outputsNonempty` | definition | Not stated. | Ours. It is the side condition for cancelling a Cartesian product of attributes. The empty attribute makes `∅ ×ˢ u = ∅ ×ˢ v`. The papers do not discuss that degeneracy. |

## Algebra proved from the definitions

These are theorems of the pair-set reading. The papers do not state them as
separate results. Where a row says “not stated”, no matching equation was
found in the two PDFs.

| Lean | Kind | Source sentence | Faithfulness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Task.ext` | theorem | Paraphrase of “a task A is a set of ordered pairs” (Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2). | Two tasks with the same pair-set are equal. This is the extensionality of that set reading, not a stated lemma. |
| `Task.IsSubtask.refl`, `.trans`, `.antisymm` | theorems | Not stated. | The subset reading of `⊑` is a partial order. Depends on the paraphrase in the `IsSubtask` row. |
| `Task.IsSubtask.union_left`, `.union_right`, `.union_le` | theorems | Not stated. The 2013 parenthetical that union “corresponds to set-theoretic union” is the only source. | Under the subset reading, `union` is the least upper bound. |
| `Task.serial_assoc` | theorem | Not stated. | Associativity of our relational `⊚`. The papers’ serial composition is a partial operation (only when `Out = In`), so this is a theorem about the extension described in the `serial` row, not about the partial operation as written. |
| `Task.serial_idOn_left`, `Task.serial_idOn_right` | theorems | Not stated. | `idOn Set.univ` is a two-sided unit for our `⊚`. |
| `Task.serial_empty_left`, `.serial_empty_right`, `Task.parallel_empty_left`, `.parallel_empty_right` | theorems | Not stated. | The empty task absorbs under both compositions, for our definitions. |
| `Task.transpose_involutive` | theorem | Not stated. It is the definition of `A~` applied twice (Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2). | `(Aᵀ)ᵀ = A`. Direct from the quoted definition. The papers do not spell out the equation. |
| `Task.transpose_serial` | theorem | Not stated. | For our `⊚`, `(A ⊚ B)ᵀ = Bᵀ ⊚ Aᵀ`. This is the usual reversal for relational composition. It is not a quoted identity, and the letter-order warning in the `serial` row applies. |
| `Task.transpose_parallel` | theorem | Not stated. | `(A ⊗ B)ᵀ = Aᵀ ⊗ Bᵀ` for our product of pair-sets. |
| `Task.IsSubtask.serial`, `.parallel`, `.transpose` | theorems | Not stated. | Our compositions and transpose are monotone in the subset order. |
| `Task.hasDisjointInputs.serial`, `.parallel` | theorems | Not stated. | Preservation results for a predicate the papers do not state. `.parallel` uses a classical split on whether two attributes are equal. |
| `Task.isFunctional.serial` | theorem | Not stated. | Our extra predicate is preserved by relational composition. |
| `Task.serial_parallel_interchange` | theorem | Not stated. | If the outputs of `A₁` and `A₂` are non-empty, `(A₁ ⊗ A₂) ⊚ (B₁ ⊗ B₂) = (A₁ ⊚ B₁) ⊗ (A₂ ⊚ B₂)`. One inclusion needs no side condition (`parallel_serial_sub_serial_parallel`). The papers do not discuss whether the two schedules are the same task. |
| `Task.exists_functional_of_transpose_not` | theorem | Deutsch 2013, §1.1, permits one input with two outputs, quoted under `Task`. | A concrete `Fin 3` task is functional and its transpose is not. This separates our predicate from transpose. It is not a claim about physics. |

## Possibility

| Lean | Kind | Source sentence | Faithfulness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Theory.SerialCompositionPrinciple` | hypothesis | Deutsch 2013, §1.2, arXiv:1210.7439. “This formalism makes it natural to conjecture – and here we come to our first constructor-theoretic law of physics, which I shall call the composition principle – that every regular network of possible tasks is a possible task.” Repeated as Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §6, principle VII, arXiv:1405.5563: “Every regular network of possible tasks is a possible task, though here we need only assume that it holds for information-processing tasks.” A regular network, 2015 §2: “a network without loops whose nodes are tasks and whose lines are their substrates, where the legitimate input states at the end of each line are the legitimate output states at its beginning.” | One serial step of our `⊚`, for every pair of tasks, not only regular networks, and not only information-processing tasks. Non-matching tasks are included; their composite may be empty. Networks with more than one link are not in the hypothesis. They are recovered only by applying it repeatedly (`serial_chain₃`). |
| `Theory.ParallelCompositionPrinciple` | hypothesis | Same composition principle, together with the parallel clause quoted under `Task.parallel`. Deutsch 2013, §1.2, also says: “Indeed, A ⊗ B may be possible even when neither A nor B is – for instance, when they violate a conservation law by equal and opposite amounts.” | Only the direction “both possible ⇒ parallel possible”. The converse direction, possible parallel with impossible factors, is not a hypothesis and we give no model of it. Open: that conservation-law example. |
| `Theory.Possible.subtask` | theorem | Not stated as a numbered result. Paraphrase of “performing the pairs of a task”. | If a constructor performs every pair of `B` and `A ⊑ B`, it performs every pair of `A`. This uses the subset reading of subtask. |
| `Theory.Impossible.of_subtask` | theorem | Not stated. | Contrapositive of the previous row. This is the pattern used when an impossibility is inherited by a larger task. The papers do not isolate it. |
| `Theory.Possible.of_serial_subtasks` | theorem | Not stated. | If `A' ⊚ B'` is possible and the factors shrink in the subset order, the smaller composite is possible. No composition principle is used. |
| `Theory.Possible.empty_of_nonempty` | theorem | Not stated. | If a substrate has some constructor, the empty task is possible. Depends on our `empty`. |
| `Theory.Impossible.of_serial_left`, `.of_serial_right` | theorems | Not stated. | Contrapositives of our serial hypothesis: an impossible composite, plus one possible factor, forces the other factor to be impossible. |
| `Theory.Possible.serial_chain₃`, `.serial_chain₃'` | theorems | Not stated. The composition principle quoted above is about every regular network, which would include a chain of three once the links match. | Two applications of our serial hypothesis. Associativity of `⊚` identifies the parenthesizations. The regular-network side condition is not re-imposed. |
| `Theory.Possible.parallel_of_serial`, `.serial_of_parallel` | theorems | Not stated. Same source as the two hypotheses. | Under both hypotheses, both schedules are possible. |
| `Theory.Possible.schedules_agree` | theorem | Not stated. | Under both hypotheses and `outputsNonempty`, the two schedules are the same task and possible. Combines the interchange theorem with the hypotheses. |
| `Theory.composition_principles_consistent` | theorem | Not a physical claim. | `omnipotent` satisfies both hypotheses, by performing every pair. `inert` also satisfies both and makes every task that contains a pair impossible (`inert_possible_iff`, `inert_impossible_of_pair`). The hypotheses do not force non-trivial tasks to be possible. |
| `Theory.possible_transpose_not_necessary` | theorem | Deutsch 2013, §3.4, arXiv:1210.7439. “The second law of thermodynamics must be reflected in patterns like A✓ & A~✘.” | The definitions allow a possible task whose transpose is impossible (`irreversible` on `Fin 3`, the task `{0} → {1}`). This is an existence result inside the logic, not a formalization of the second law. |
| `Theory.serialBreak_not_serial` | theorem | Not stated. | A theory that performs `{0} → {1}` and `{1} → {2}` on `Fin 3`, and nothing else, does not satisfy the serial hypothesis. Shows the hypothesis is not a theorem of the definitions. |
| `Theory.parallelBreak_not_parallel` | theorem | Not stated. | A theory with constructors only for `Fin 3` does not satisfy the parallel hypothesis, because `Fin 3` is not `Fin 3 × Fin 3` (`fin3_ne_prod`). The encoding `PLift (S = Fin 3)` is a device of the model, not a notion from the papers. |

## Stated in the sources and not formalized

| Gap | Source sentence | What is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Principle I | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “I. All other laws of physics are expressible entirely in terms of statements about which physical transformations are possible and which are impossible, and why.” | No statement that subsidiary theories must have this form. `Possible` and `Impossible` are the vocabulary such statements would use. |
| Principle II (locality) | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, arXiv:1405.5563. “II. There exists a mode of description such that the state of the combined system S₁ ⊕ S₂ of any two substrates S₁ and S₂ is the ordered pair (x, y) of the states x of S₁ and y of S₂, and any construction undergone by S₁ and not S₂ can change only x and not y.” | The product type matches only the “ordered pair of states” clause. The clause that a construction on one factor leaves the other unchanged is not a theorem. |
| Intrinsic attributes | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2. “An intrinsic attribute of a substrate is one that does not refer to any other specific substrate.” Deutsch 2013, §1.1: “A construction task specifies only intrinsic attributes of its substrates, not extrinsic ones such as the name or location of a particular instance.” | Tasks may use any set of states. Names and locations are simply not in the type. |
| Isolation | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2. “where the constructor and the substrates jointly are isolated.” Deutsch 2013, §1.1: a constructor is characterised by its effect “if it and its substrates jointly constituted a closed system.” | No isolation or closed-system condition. |
| Side-effects | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2. “A task A is possible with side-effects, which we write as A✓ [with a subscript], if (A ⊗ T)✓ for some task T on some generic, naturally occurring substrate.” | The bracketed words replace a subscript that the PDF text did not recover. The defined notion is not formalized. The quotation of the formula `(A ⊗ T)✓` is the recovered prose. |
| Accuracy and limits | Deutsch & Marletto 2015, §2, the sentence after the definition of possible, requiring “a measure of the accuracy” and a meaning for convergence of “an infinite sequence of tasks A₁, A₂, … ” to a limiting task. Deutsch 2013, §3.14, introduces Aε. | Possibility is an exact pair-set. No ε, no metric, no limit of tasks. |
| Arbitrary regular networks | The composition principle quoted under `SerialCompositionPrinciple`. | Only one serial step and one parallel step are hypothesized. A general network is not a defined object. |
| Possible composite of impossible tasks | Deutsch 2013, §1.2, quoted under `ParallelCompositionPrinciple`. | Allowed by the definition of `Possible`, since nothing forbids it, but not illustrated by a model. |
